How this is measured

  • Each person answers 6 scenario questions before a short lesson, then 6 parallel questions after it. Each question has one correct choice and several common practices (tourniquet, ojha, cutting…).
  • There are two question sets (A and B). Each person gets a random order (A then B, or B then A), so an easier set can't pass for learning. The split is shown below.
  • A "dangerous choice" is a choice the Government of India guidelines name as harmful, such as a tourniquet, a healer, cutting or sucking, or waiting.
  • Only completed checks count. A device that completes more than 5 checks in a day only counts for its first 5.
  • Limits: this measures what people would do immediately after the lesson, not months later, and not in a real emergency. People who open a shared link are self-selected and are not a random sample of rural India.
